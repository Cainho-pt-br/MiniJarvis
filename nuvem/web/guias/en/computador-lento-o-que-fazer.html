Slow computer: what to do (without formatting)

Five simple checks for Windows and Mac that fix most cases of a slow computer, without a technician or formatting.

1. See what is slowing it down

Most of the time your computer isn't old: there is a program eating up memory. On Windows, press Ctrl+Shift+Esc to open Task Manager and click the Memory column. On Mac, open Activity Monitor. A browser with 40 tabs, duplicate antivirus software, and "optimizer" apps are the top culprits.

2. Check free disk space

With less than 10% free space, everything slows down because the system uses the disk as extra memory. On Windows: Settings → System → Storage. On Mac: Apple menu → About This Mac → Storage.

3. Clean temporary files

Temporary files accumulate gigabytes. On Windows, type %temp% in the search bar, open the folder, and delete anything older than a week. Be careful not to touch Documents, Pictures, or Downloads by mistake.

4. Remove programs from startup

Many apps launch when you turn on your computer without you needing them. In Task Manager, go to the Startup tab and disable what you don't use every day (keep your antivirus and audio driver enabled).

5. Do a real restart

On Windows, "Shut down" doesn't always shut down completely: the system hibernates. Use Restart once a week. If it is still slow after all this, it may have a mechanical hard drive (HDD): upgrading to an SSD is the single most noticeable improvement you can make.
